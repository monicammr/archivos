\snote{scen}

This note describes in detail three aspects of the computations that the main text and the other notes only summarise: how the random scenarios treat parameters whose nominal values are negative, zero or extremely small; how the problems with the most expensive simulations were computed, and how the results were checked across machines; and why the greedy search of the method does not reach an admissible subset in Lang although one exists.

\subsection*{Construction of the random scenarios}
Every random scenario used by the method (selection, admissibility test, robustness, the admissibility test applied to the other criteria and the error in units of the noise) has the form
\[ \theta^{(k)}_j=\theta_{0,j}\,\bigl(1+\varepsilon\,u^{(k)}_j\bigr),\qquad u^{(k)}_j\sim\mathcal U(-1,1),\quad k=1,\dots,15, \]
with the directions $u^{(k)}$ drawn from a generator with fixed seed (42), so that all these analyses use exactly the same scenarios and their comparisons are paired. Because $|\varepsilon u^{(k)}_j|\le\varepsilon\le0.5$, the factor $1+\varepsilon u^{(k)}_j$ lies in $[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\subset(0,2)$: every parameter keeps its sign and changes by at most a fraction $\varepsilon$ of its value. No clipping or lower bound is applied to the perturbed values, so that the relative size of the change is the same for every parameter whatever the magnitude or sign of its nominal value. Three groups of parameters deserve comment:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Negative nominal values.} Eleven estimated parameters have negative nominal values: \texttt{alpha\_cer} in Armistead, a factor of the ceramide feedback, and ten parameters of Raimundez that describe differences between the two cell lines on a linear scale. They are perturbed by $\pm\varepsilon$ of their value like the others, and in every re-estimation their sign is fixed and $\log|\theta_j|$ is optimised within $[\log(|\theta_{0,j}|/10),\log(10|\theta_{0,j}|)]$.
\item \emph{Very small positive values.} Lang has 44 estimated parameters with nominal values below $10^{-12}$ (initial amounts and rate constants estimated on a logarithmic scale, with lower bound $10^{-24}$ in the benchmark). They are perturbed relatively, like all others, so their perturbations have the same relative size.
\item \emph{Zero nominal values.} Six estimated parameters have nominal value zero (four in Lang, two in Raimundez). The simulator represents them by $10^{-12}$; their relative perturbations, and therefore their columns of the relative sensitivity matrix $J$ and their finite-perturbation effects, are negligible, so our method never selects them. Pivoted QR, which normalises every column of its sensitivity matrix to unit length and uses an absolute finite-difference step for very small values, can select them: one of its two parameters in Raimundez (\texttt{d\_MAPKtotal\_\_MKN1\_2\_HS746T}) has nominal value zero.
\end{itemize}
A scenario is discarded when the simulation fails or when the change of the full model is negligible ($\|\Delta y_{\mathrm{full}}\|<\max(10^{-10}\|y(\theta_0)\|,10^{-6})$), and the number of valid scenarios is reported in every table.

\subsection*{Computation of the problems with expensive simulations}
Eight problems have simulations slow enough to make the repeated nonlinear re-estimation of the admissibility test impractical (Alkan, Bachmann, Beer, Chen, Isensee, Lang, Lucarelli and Raimundez); for them $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is computed to first order (Methods), and six of them are admissible. These problems, and the analyses that need many fits of a large model, were computed on shared servers with 128 and 256 cores; the others on an 8-core workstation. The following implementation details matter for reproducing the results:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Parallelisation.} The columns of $J$ and the stage-2 ordering by finite effects are distributed over processes; in the nested validation the finite-difference Jacobian of each fit of the full model is distributed as well (70 processes in Raimundez, which reduced the computation from hours to about a minute), and in the identifiability analysis the points of the profiles are computed in parallel.
\item \emph{Caches.} For problems with more than 200 parameters, $J$, the stage-2 ordering and the $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ of the subsets already evaluated are stored on disk so that an interrupted run can resume. Each setting of the method (Supplementary Note \emph{Sensitivity to the method settings}) uses its own cache, because these quantities depend on the finite-difference step, the integrator tolerances and the perturbation size.
\item \emph{Full model.} The full model is re-estimated on real data in every admissible problem (up to 155 free parameters, in Chen), and in the synthetic experiment only when it has at most 100 free parameters once those acting only in test conditions are removed, which excludes Chen and Raimundez. All real-data numbers of the paper come from a single run, the nested validation (Supplementary Note \emph{Nested validation without the $\theta_0$ anchor}).
\item \emph{Checkpoints.} Long fits write checkpoints identified by the problem and the number of free parameters. When an analysis is repeated with a different subset of the same size, the results and checkpoints of that problem must be deleted first; otherwise an earlier fit would be resumed. Likewise, the admissibility test of the subsets of the other criteria must run after their selections (pivoted QR, Brun, Morris, profile likelihood) have been computed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Agreement between machines}
The selection of Lang and Raimundez was computed independently on the workstation and on the 128-core server, with the same code and NumPy (2.4.6) and SciPy (1.17.1) but different versions of the simulator (libRoadRunner 2.9.2 on the workstation and 2.10.0 on the server): both runs gave the same subsets, and the median $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ agreed to within $10^{-8}$. All the analyses of Armistead were also computed on both machines; the test $\chi^2/n$ of the reduced model in the nested validation agreed to within $10^{-8}$ (1.2181).

\subsection*{Dependence of the greedy search on its path: Lang}
In Lang, an oscillating cell-cycle model with 294 estimated parameters (277 of which affect the outputs) simulated to $t=136{,}472$, the method stops without an admissible subset. Stage~1 does not reach the energy threshold $\tau_R$ (the 13 parameters accepted by the conditioning test keep 17\% of the sensitivity energy), so stage~2 orders the parameters by their finite effect. The first parameter is \texttt{kDeE2f1} (degradation of E2F1) and the second \texttt{kDeCe}; the pair is not admissible, and the search keeps adding the next parameters in this order as long as the conditioning test is satisfied, until no further parameter can be added ($\kappa=8.7$, VIF $=9.99$, just below the limit of 10). The resulting 13 parameters have a median first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ of 56\%, above $\tau_e$. Because adding parameters can never increase the first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ (Lean, \texttt{fitErr\_union\_le}), no subset of these 13 parameters is admissible either.

Admissible pairs nevertheless exist outside this path. With the same scenarios and only the conditioning threshold changed (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:settingsextra}), the search accepts a different second parameter and finds \{\texttt{kDeE2f1}, \texttt{kSySkp2}\} ($e_{\mathrm{fit}}=13\%$, with $\tau_\kappa=\tau_{\mathrm{VIF}}=5$) and \{\texttt{kDeE2f1}, \texttt{kSyCb1}\} (16\%, with 30); with finite-difference steps of 0.1\% or 5\% it finds \{\texttt{kDeE2f1}, \texttt{kAspEB55}\} and \{\texttt{kDeE2f1}, \texttt{kSyCb1}\}. With perturbations of $\pm1\%$, or with coarser integrator tolerances ($10^{-6}/10^{-8}$), the pair \{\texttt{kDeE2f1}, \texttt{kDeCe}\} itself becomes admissible (8\% and 13\%); this last result shows that the first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ of this long oscillatory simulation is sensitive to the accuracy of the integration, which agrees with the discrepancy between our simulation of Lang and the reference simulation of the benchmark (Supplementary Note \emph{Verification of the simulator}). Lang is therefore non-admissible under the method as defined, but the failure reflects the greedy nature of the search, which commits to the second parameter with the largest finite effect, and not the absence of a small admissible subset; a search that explored several candidates for each position (for example a beam search) would find one. We kept the method unchanged and report Lang as not admissible.

\subsection*{Settings variants of Raimundez}
Raimundez, with 136 estimated parameters of which 79 are calibration parameters, selects \{\texttt{kdeg\_membran\_\_MKN1}, \texttt{ka\_MAPK\_\_MKN1}\} in every computed variant of the settings, with a median $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ between 18\% and 19\%; the selection of this problem is insensitive to the settings. Two variants of Lang ($\pm10\%$ and tolerances $10^{-9}/10^{-12}$) and one of Raimundez ($\tau_e=0.10$) did not finish in the available computing time.
