\subsection*{Question}
The method selects a subset $S$ that is \emph{sufficient}: re-estimating $S$ together with the calibration parameters $\Theta_C$, with the other parameters at $\theta_0$, reproduces the outputs of the full model. Sufficiency does not imply that the data determine the values of the selected parameters. A selected parameter could, for example, compensate for a scaling factor of the measurement, so that only their product is determined. This note examines whether the selected parameters are also practically identifiable in the reduced problem, that is, with $S\cup\Theta_C$ free and the remaining parameters at their published values.

\subsection*{Design}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Local check.} At $\theta_0$, the columns of the sensitivity matrix for $S\cup\Theta_C$, normalised to unit length and not centred (the Fisher information of the reduced problem up to scaling), were examined: for each selected parameter, the variance inflation factor (VIF) within $S\cup\Theta_C$ and the column of $\Theta_C$ or $S$ most similar to it.
\item \emph{Profile likelihood of the reduced problem.} With all measurements of the problem and the noise levels of the benchmark, the reduced problem was fitted from $\theta_0$ (minimum $\hat\theta$). For each selected parameter $\theta_j$, $\log|\theta_j|$ was fixed at displacements of 0.05 to 4.6 from $\log|\hat\theta_j|$ and the other parameters of $S\cup\Theta_C$ were re-estimated, with the same least-squares method and bounds $[|\theta_0|/10,10|\theta_0|]$ as in the rest of the study. If a profile point improved the minimum, the reduced problem was re-fitted from that point and the profile repeated (at most three rounds). Only points within the bounds of $\theta_j$ were used. As in Raue et al.\ (2009), $\theta_j$ is \emph{identifiable} if $\Delta\chi^2$ exceeds 3.84 (95\%, one degree of freedom) on both sides within the bounds, \emph{practically non-identifiable} if on one side only, and \emph{flat} otherwise. Because several problems have recorded noise levels that do not match the scatter of the data, a robust variant scales the threshold by $\max(1,\chi^2_{\min}/\mathrm{d.f.})$, which is equivalent to rescaling the noise so that the fit has $\chi^2/\mathrm{d.f.}=1$.
\item \emph{Condition of the formal theorem.} For each selected parameter we computed the quantities that appear in the theorem described below: the profiled information $I_j=1/[(A^{\top}A)^{-1}]_{jj}$, where $A$ is the sensitivity matrix of the residuals with respect to $\log|\theta|$ for $S\cup\Theta_C$ at $\hat\theta$; the residual gradient $\gamma=\|P r_0\|$, the norm of the projection of the residual at $\hat\theta$ onto the range of $A$; and the nonlinear remainder $\|r(\hat\theta+\delta)-r_0-A\delta\|$ at the computed profile points.
\end{enumerate}
Five problems with expensive simulations (Bachmann, Chen, Isensee, Raimundez, Smith) were computed on a server (Raimundez on a 128-core server, the others on a 256-core server) and the other 26 on an 8-core workstation, so the profiles cover all 31 problems with an admissible subset.

\subsection*{Results}
Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identif} summarises the results for the 31 problems with an admissible subset, Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:identifperfiles} shows the profile of every selected parameter in every problem, and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identifparam} gives, for every parameter, its confidence interval and the quantities of the formal condition ($I_j$, the Wald half-width, $\gamma$, $\varepsilon$ and the distances to the bounds).

\emph{Profile likelihood.} In 25 of the 31 problems all selected parameters were practically identifiable, and 65 of the 74 selected parameters were identifiable (72 of them could be evaluated). The exceptions are of three kinds. In Fujita a selected parameter is confounded with a measurement scaling factor: the columns of \texttt{reaction\_6\_k1} and \texttt{scaling\_pS6\_tot} have cosine 1.00, and neither selected parameter is identifiable. In Giordano and Perelson the recorded noise levels are far larger than the residuals ($\chi^2/n<10^{-2}$), so no parameter change raises $\chi^2$ by 3.84; in Okuonghae and Oliveira one parameter is identifiable and the profile of the other could not be simulated. In Smith, the optimum of \texttt{k8} lies at the lower bound of the study. With the robust threshold, 23 of the 31 problems keep all their parameters identifiable (60 of the 74 parameters): Raia ($\chi^2/\mathrm{d.f.}=1{,}791$) and Chen (168) lose one or both, and so does the second parameter of Smith (1{,}894), because their recorded noise levels are too small for the scatter of the data.

\emph{Local check.} A VIF above 10 within $S\cup\Theta_C$ flags a correlation between a selected parameter and a calibration parameter, but it does not by itself make the parameter non-identifiable: in Brannmark, Fiedler, Isensee, Rahman, Raia, Raimundez, Schwen and Weber the VIF exceeds 10, yet all their selected parameters are identifiable in the profiles, because the data are informative enough to separate them. Only in Fujita does the confounding reach the profile.

\emph{Condition of the theorem.} The linear condition ($\varepsilon=0$, $\gamma=0$) agrees with the profile classification for all 72 parameters whose profile could be evaluated (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identif}, column Wald): it holds for the 65 identifiable parameters and fails for the other seven. With the nonlinear remainder evaluated at the profile points, the hypotheses of the theorem hold for 24 parameters (column Lean), and never for a parameter whose profile is not identifiable. The quantitative certificate is therefore conservative: the profiled information $I_j$ explains which parameters are identifiable, and the nonlinearity of the models is what prevents a certificate over the whole range of the study.

\emph{Cost of the identifiability check.} The last column of Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identif} gives the computing time needed to determine the identifiability of the selected parameters of each problem: one fit of the reduced problem and the re-estimations at the 40 profile points of its two selected parameters (more for Bruno, Lucarelli and Zhao). Because only the selected and calibration parameters are free, each re-estimation is small. The median over the 31 problems was 34~s of computing time; 20 problems needed less than a minute and 24 less than ten minutes. The check took hours only where a single simulation is slow (Chen, 44~s per simulation on the server: 15~h) or where many calibration parameters are re-estimated at every point (Raimundez, 79: 4.0~h; Isensee: 4.3~h). The profile points are independent, so on many cores the elapsed time is a fraction of these values (Chen took about 3~h on the server). These include problems in which the profile likelihood of the full model takes hours (Bachmann, 40~h of computing time) or was not run (Chen, Raimundez; \sref{profile}), so the identifiability of the selected subset can be verified at a fraction of that cost. The times were measured on three machines (an 8-core workstation and shared servers with 128 and 256 cores) and are observed times, not benchmarks.

\subsection*{Subsets that are both sufficient and identifiable}
Admissibility and practical identifiability are different properties (see \emph{Relation with sufficiency} above), so a subset that can be used to reduce the model and whose parameters are also determined by the data has to satisfy both. For a problem with selected subset $S$ and calibration parameters $\Theta_C$ we distinguish four nested conditions, each checked for every parameter of $S$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Reducible (admissible).} Re-estimating $S\cup\Theta_C$ with the other parameters at $\theta_0$ reproduces the full model: the median $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ over the 15 scenarios is at most $\tau_e=\sqrt{1-c^2}=0.436$ (Theorems~1 and~2 of the main text and steps 1--22 of Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:modules}).
\item \emph{Identifiable by the profiles.} For every $\theta_j$, $j\in S$, the profile of the reduced problem exceeds $\Delta\chi^2=3.84$ on both sides within the bounds (the predicate \texttt{PracticallyIdentifiable}).
\item \emph{Linear condition.} $\sqrt{c/I_j}<\min(d_-,d_+)$ for every $j\in S$ (Proposition~\ref{p:wald} with $\gamma=\varepsilon=0$): the Wald interval is inside the bounds.
\item \emph{Hypotheses of the theorem.} $t^{\ast}_j<\min(d_-,d_+)$ for every $j\in S$, with $I_j$, $\gamma$ (Proposition~\ref{p:proj}) and $\|r_0\|$ computed at the optimum and $\varepsilon$ the largest nonlinear remainder at the computed profile points; by Corollary~\ref{c:ident} every parameter of $S$ is then practically identifiable. Because $\varepsilon$ is evaluated at points rather than bounded over the whole admissible set, this is a numerical check of the hypotheses, not a proof for the particular model.
\end{enumerate}
Of the 34 problems, 31 satisfy condition~1. Of these, 25 also satisfy condition~2 for all their selected parameters (23 with the robust threshold); the six exceptions are listed under \emph{Profile likelihood} above. Condition~3 holds for all selected parameters in 27 problems: the 25 of condition~2 and Okuonghae and Oliveira, in which the profile of one parameter could not be simulated, so that the linear condition cannot be compared with it. Condition~4 holds for all selected parameters in seven problems, Armistead ($|S|=2$), Blasi (2), Boehm (2), Bruno (5), Rahman (2), Sneyd (2) and Zheng (2), none of which has calibration parameters. The models of these seven problems are also among the 28 verified in Lean (Supplementary Note \textit{Verified benchmark models}), so for them every link is in place, with different status: the hypotheses on the dynamics are proved in Lean (at the nominal parameters and in the base condition), whereas the admissibility of the subset and the hypotheses of the identifiability theorem are checked numerically at the computed points, not certified. In the other 24 admissible problems the subset is reducible and, in 18 of them, identifiable by the profiles, but condition~4 fails for at least one parameter: in Fujita, Giordano, Perelson and Smith because the information $I_j$ is already insufficient (condition~3 fails), and in the others because of the nonlinear remainder $\varepsilon$ (in Raimundez, for example, both parameters satisfy condition~3 but the remainder reaches 215 and 241 noise units, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identifparam}).

\subsection*{Scope}
The result is practical identifiability of the reduced problem: local, within the bounds of the study, with the non-selected parameters at $\theta_0$ and with the noise model of the benchmark. It is not identifiability of the full model, in which all parameters are free: in the profile likelihood of the full model only 20 of the 52 selected parameters evaluated were identifiable (\sref{profile}). That contrast is consistent with the purpose of the method. The data do not determine all the parameters of these models, but, with the other parameters at their published values, a few selected parameters are both sufficient to reproduce the outputs and, in most problems, determined by the data. Identifiability does not imply that the estimated values are the true biological values: they depend on the values at which the other parameters are fixed.
