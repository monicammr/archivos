\snote{synth}

With real data the true parameters are unknown, so the test $\chi^2/n$ of Figure~3 measures agreement with noisy data, not with the true response. To measure the prediction error itself we repeated the external validation with synthetic data generated from a known truth.

\subsection*{Design}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Truth.} With a fixed seed (NumPy \texttt{default\_rng(42)}) a vector $u\in[-1,1]^p$ with components $u_j\sim\mathcal U(-1,1)$ is drawn and $\theta^\ast_j=\theta_{0,j}(1+0.2\,u_j)$ for every parameter that can be estimated from the training data (parameters acting only in test conditions stay at $\theta_{0,j}$, since no method could learn them).
\item \emph{Data.} $\tilde y_i=y_i(\theta^\ast)+\epsilon_i$ with $\epsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ in noise-scaled units, i.e.\ Gaussian noise with the standard deviation $\sigma_i$ recorded in the benchmark, for every measurement of every condition.
\item \emph{Split and fits.} The same training/test split as for the real data (main text, Methods). The reduced model (re-estimating $S\cup\Theta_C$) and the full model (re-estimating all parameters, except for Lang) are fitted to the synthetic training data starting from $\theta_0$, with the same least-squares method and bounds.
\item \emph{Prediction error.} On the test measurements,
\[ e_{\mathrm{pred}}=\frac{\|y_{\mathrm{test}}(\hat\theta)-y_{\mathrm{test}}(\theta^\ast)\|}{\|y_{\mathrm{test}}(\theta^\ast)-y_{\mathrm{test}}(\theta_0)\|}, \]
the error of the prediction relative to the change of the true response; $e_{\mathrm{pred}}=1$ for the published estimate $\theta_0$, and $e_{\mathrm{pred}}\le\tau_e=0.436$ is used as the success criterion.
\item \emph{Signal and noise.} When the change from $\theta_0$ to $\theta^\ast$ moves the test outputs less than the noise, $e_{\mathrm{pred}}$ measures only noise. The signal is considered larger than the noise when the test $\chi^2/n$ of $\theta_0$ on the synthetic data is at least 2.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Results}
The experiment could be evaluated in 29 of the 32 admissible problems (Bertozzi and Blasi have no training/test split; for Isensee the simulation of $\theta^\ast$ failed). In 15 problems the signal was below the noise; in 13 of these the reduced model predicted at the noise level (test $\chi^2/n\le2$). In the 14 problems where the signal exceeded the noise, the reduced model reached $e_{\mathrm{pred}}\le\tau_e$ in 9, and its $e_{\mathrm{pred}}$ was no larger than that of the full model in 7 of the 13 problems where the full model was also fitted (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:synthetic}).
