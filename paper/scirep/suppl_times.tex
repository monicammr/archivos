\snote{time}

This note reports the computing time of every analysis of the paper, problem by problem, with the purpose of showing what it costs to select and verify a subset with the method, how that cost compares with the standard identifiability analysis, profile likelihood of the full model, and where the computing time of the whole study was spent.

\subsection*{How the times were measured}
Each program records, for every problem, the elapsed time of its computation (field \texttt{segundos} of the result files of the reproducibility package). Several analyses use parallelism within a problem: the selection distributes the columns of $J$ and the stage-2 ordering over 16 to 64 processes; the nested validation distributes the finite-difference Jacobian of each fit of the full model; profile likelihood of the full model computes all points of a problem in parallel on up to 120 cores. Other analyses consist of many independent single-core tasks (the seeds of the synthetic experiment, the scenarios and seeds of the admissibility test of the other criteria, the points of the identifiability profiles); for them the tables give the sum over the tasks, which is processor time and is larger than the elapsed time. The problems with expensive simulations were computed on shared servers with 128 and 256 cores and the others on an 8-core workstation; the times on shared servers are upper bounds, because other computations ran at the same time. The complete method was timed in a run from scratch, without cached sensitivities (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tiempos} compares it with profile likelihood). Profile likelihood of the full model is given both as elapsed time, with the points of a problem in parallel, and as processor time, the sum over its points; only the processor time is added to the totals. The per-problem tables give each time in seconds, minutes or hours, whichever is most readable; the totals are given in hours. For the six problems selected with the first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$, the nonlinear re-estimation of the final subset is listed separately (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timereview}), because it is not part of the timed selection.

\subsection*{What the times show}
\emph{Selecting a subset is affordable.} The complete method took a median of 19~s per problem and less than ten minutes in 32 of the 34 problems; the slowest were Chen (33~min, 35~s per simulation), Smith (19~min) and Isensee (10~min). On one core the same computation took at most about three times longer (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timemethod}). Where the selection used the first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$, verifying the final subset by nonlinear re-estimation was considerably more expensive than the selection itself: 12.8~h of processor time for Chen, 4.1~h for Raimundez and 3.9~h for Isensee, against 33, 2 and 10~min for the selection.

\emph{Verifying it is affordable too.} Determining the identifiability of the selected parameters (one fit of the reduced problem and the re-estimations at the profile points) took a median of 34~s per problem, and it was feasible in all 31 admissible problems, including those in which profile likelihood of the full model took hours or was not run (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timevalidation}). Profile likelihood of the full model, the standard identifiability analysis, took a median of 17~s of elapsed time in the 28 problems in which it was run (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timecomparison}), so on small problems it is as fast as our method, which was faster than its first round in 12 of these 28 problems; it took 70~min for Bachmann and 11~min for Lucarelli (40 and 9~h of processor time) and 5.2~h for Isensee (110~h of processor time), and it was not run for Chen and Raimundez, for Blasi, which has no training/test split, or for the three problems without an admissible subset (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tiempos}), in which the method and the identifiability check of the reduced problem still ran in minutes to hours.

\emph{Where the computing time went.} All analyses reported in the paper together took about 673 hours of processor time (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timebreakdown}, in hours and grouped by purpose; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:timesummary}, per analysis). The method itself cost about 23~h for the 34 problems: 1.3~h for the selection and 21.3~h for the nonlinear verification of the final subset, almost all of it in Chen, Raimundez and Isensee. The remaining ${\sim}650$~h were spent on the evaluation of the method (243~h) and on the comparison with other criteria (408~h, including 39~h for the identifiability profiles of the subsets of the other criteria), which were carried out for this paper and are not needed to apply the method. This is the sum over all cores of the times recorded by each task in the final runs, whose results are those of the reproducibility package; earlier runs superseded by corrections of the code, repeated runs and the compilation of the Lean proofs are not included. Because the analyses ran in parallel on servers with 88 to 256 cores and on an 8-core workstation, the elapsed time was much shorter than the processor time. Most of the time was spent in analyses that re-estimate models many times and that are not part of the method: profile likelihood of the full model (193~h, 110~h of it for Isensee), the identifiability profiles (26~h, and 102~h more for the check with 20 points per side), the synthetic experiments (44~h over the three amplitudes and 86~h for the log-uniform and sparse truths of all criteria), the tests of the subsets of all criteria (86~h), the variants of the method settings (50~h, because each variant reruns the complete method) and the nonlinear check of the first-order problems (21~h). The check of the identifiability hypotheses over the admissible set did not record times per problem and is not included; it took about 3~h of elapsed time on an 88-core server. Five problems with slow simulations or many parameters, Isensee, Chen, Raimundez, Smith and Bachmann, account for 87\% of the total, whereas the median over the problems of the time of all analyses together is about one hour.
