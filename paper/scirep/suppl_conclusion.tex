\section*{Concluding remarks}

This Supplementary Information was written so that every claim of the main text can be traced to its source and checked independently. Supplementary Tables S1 and S2 give the subsets selected for each benchmark problem and the notation of the method, and Supplementary Note~1 compares our simulation of the measured outputs with the reference simulations of the benchmark. These materials connect the results reported in the paper with the data and code from which they were obtained.

The remaining material documents the formal verification and, just as importantly, its scope. Supplementary Figure~S1 and Table~S4 separate three levels of evidence: general mathematical guarantees proved in Lean~4 (G1--G4), hypotheses proved for specific models (G5), and numerical conditions computed in Python, on which the application of the guarantees to a particular result depends. Supplementary Table~S3 and Note~2 follow the 31 modules of the formalisation in the order in which they depend on each other, from the energy bound on the error of the reduced model and the relation between $\cos\Delta$ and the best rescaled fit, through the differentiability of ODE solutions with respect to the parameters, to certificates for ODE models and the hypotheses of the benchmark models; they also state what the proofs do not establish, such as identifiability of the nonlinear models or the convergence of the numerical optimisation. Supplementary Note~3 and Table~S5 give the statement and meaning of each of the 289 theorems and lemmas, so that every formal statement can be compared with the mathematics it encodes. Supplementary Note~4 and Table~S6 show, model by model, the data on which the Lean kernel executed its checks, a complete worked example, an independent check that the translated equations agree with the SBML models, and the reasons why seven benchmark problems were not verified. Supplementary Note~5 explains how to reproduce all of this. Finally, Supplementary Note~6, Table~S7 and Figure~S2 document the social cognitive theory model that motivated the method: a small model with real data in which 22 parameters fit the data equally well with widely different values, and in which two parameters selected by the method reproduce the model and predict unseen days better than the full model.

Taken together, the formalisation establishes that the steps of the method are mathematically sound under explicit hypotheses and that 28 benchmark models satisfy the hypotheses on the dynamics; the numerical quantities reported in the paper are computed in floating point by the accompanying Python code, and the formal results state precisely which conclusions follow from them. Everything described here can be reproduced from the repository: the proofs compile with a single command, the supplementary tables and notes are generated from the Lean sources and the result files, and the analyses can be rerun from the published benchmark. We hope that this combination of a public benchmark, open code and machine-checked mathematics, together with a clear statement of what each part guarantees, makes the reduction method easy to verify, to reuse and to extend to other dynamical models.
