\snote{concl}

This Supplementary Information was written so that every claim of the main text can be traced to its source and checked independently. Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:selected} and~\ref{tab:notation} give the subsets selected for each benchmark problem and the notation of the method, and \sref{sim} compares our simulation of the measured outputs with the reference simulations of the benchmark. \sref{val} and Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:valscatter} show the prediction of held-out experiments on an absolute scale. These materials connect the results reported in the paper with the data and code from which they were obtained.

The remaining material documents the formal verification and, just as importantly, its scope. Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:scope} separate three levels of evidence: general mathematical guarantees proved in Lean~4 (G1--G4 and G6), hypotheses proved for specific models (G5), and numerical conditions computed in Python, on which the application of the guarantees to a particular result depends. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:modules} and \sref{seq} follow the 32 modules of the formalisation in the order in which they depend on each other, from the energy bound on the error of the reduced model and the relation between $\cos\Delta$ and the best rescaled fit, through the differentiability of ODE solutions with respect to the parameters, to certificates for ODE models and the hypotheses of the benchmark models; they also state what the proofs do not establish, such as identifiability of the full nonlinear models or the convergence of the numerical optimisation; for the selected parameters of the reduced problem, step~32 gives a sufficient condition for practical identifiability whose hypotheses are checked numerically at the computed profile points (\sref{identif}). \sref{stmts} and Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:leannot} and~\ref{tab:statements} give the statement and meaning of each of the 309 theorems and lemmas, so that every formal statement can be compared with the mathematics it encodes. \sref{models} and Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:models} and~\ref{tab:boehm} show, model by model, the data on which the Lean kernel executed its checks, a complete worked example, an independent check that the translated equations agree with the SBML models, and the reasons why seven benchmark problems were not verified. \sref{repro} explains how to reproduce all of this. The numerical analyses cited in the main text are documented in full: \sref{sim} compares our simulator with the reference simulations of the benchmark, \sref{robust} gives the distribution of the fitting error over the paired random scenarios at every perturbation size, \sref{synth} reports the prediction of held-out data when the truth is known, \sref{single} examines the minimum subset size of two parameters, a design choice (a single parameter is already admissible in 15 of 27 problems), \sref{settings} gives the effect of every setting of the method on every problem, and \sref{cost} the computational cost. \sref{systems} gives a summary of each of the 34 benchmark problems: what the model describes, what the selected parameters mean in its reactions, how well the reduced model reproduces the full one and how it predicted held-out data.

Taken together, the formalisation establishes that the steps of the method are mathematically sound under explicit hypotheses and that 28 benchmark models satisfy the hypotheses on the dynamics; the numerical quantities reported in the paper are computed in floating point by the accompanying Python code, and the formal results state precisely which conclusions follow from them. Everything described here can be reproduced from the repository: the proofs compile with a single command, the supplementary tables and notes are generated from the Lean sources and the result files, and the analyses can be rerun from the published benchmark. We hope that this combination of a public benchmark, open code and machine-checked mathematics, together with a clear statement of what each part guarantees, makes the reduction method easy to verify, to reuse and to extend to other dynamical models.
