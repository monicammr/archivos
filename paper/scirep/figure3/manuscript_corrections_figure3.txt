  NEW (LaTeX):

The reduced model predicted the held-out experiments as well as or better than the full model (test $\chi^2/n$ within 5\% of the full model or lower) in 27 of the 28 problems in which both could be compared (Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}a, which shows the 25 problems with noisy data); in 5 of them (Borghans, Isensee, Perelson, Smith, Zhao) the two test errors differed by less than 0.2\%. The exception was Raia (test $\chi^2/n$ of 8.2 versus 5.1). In several problems the full model overfitted the training data and predicted much worse, for example Brannmark (test $\chi^2/n$ 5.8 versus 2,145), Weber (1.0 versus 226), Zheng (1.6 versus 20.6) and Bachmann (4.8 versus 12.2). Compared with the published estimate $\theta_0$, which was obtained using all data including the test set, the reduced model reached a test $\chi^2/n$ no larger than twice that of $\theta_0$ in 23 of 30 problems (21 of the 27 shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}b). In 5 problems (Chen, Okuonghae, Oliveira, Salazar-Cavazos and Smith) even $\theta_0$ has $\chi^2/n>10$, which indicates that the noise levels recorded in the benchmark do not match the scatter of the data; in these problems a favourable ratio does not imply a good absolute fit.
