\snote{systems}

This note summarises, for each of the 34 benchmark problems, what the model describes, which parameters the method selected and what they mean in the model, how well the reduced model reproduces the full one, and how it predicted held-out data. The meaning of each selected parameter was taken from the reactions and rules of the SBML model in which it appears (following assignment rules and condition-specific parameters to the reactions they affect). The numbers are those of Table~1 of the main text and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:selected}: $p$ is the number of estimated parameters, $\kappa$ and VIF are the condition number and largest variance inflation factor of the selected sensitivity columns, $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ is the fraction of sensitivity energy kept, $\cos\Delta$ and $e_{\mathrm{fit}}$ are medians over 15 random scenarios at $\pm$5\% (threshold $\tau_e=0.436$), the level of guarantee is defined in the Results of the main text, and the robustness patterns A--D are those of Fig.~5 of the main text. In the external validation, both the reduced and the full model are refitted to the training data, and the test $\chi^2/n$ (mean squared residual divided by the recorded noise variance; about 1 for a model that predicts within the noise) is computed on the held-out data. The published estimate $\theta_0$ was obtained with all the data, including the test data, so its test $\chi^2/n$ is a reference rather than a competing prediction, and the reduced model, whose non-selected parameters stay at $\theta_0$, inherits part of that information (\sref{nested}).
