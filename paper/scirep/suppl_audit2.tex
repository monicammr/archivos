\section*{Supplementary Note: Conditioning with unit-length columns}

The selection tests $\kappa$ and VIF on columns of $J_S$ that are centred and scaled to unit variance (the correlation matrix of the sensitivity columns), whereas the Lean statements on the linearised least-squares problem use columns scaled to unit length without centring. Both are antitone in the sense required by the greedy selection, so the selection itself is not affected; the statements on the linearised problem, however, apply only when the unit-length values satisfy the bounds. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:conditioning} gives both for every final subset (\texttt{analysis/conditioning\_check.py}; central differences with step 1\%, $2|S|+2|\Theta_C|$ simulations per problem). They agree closely except in Rahman, where the unit-length VIF is 39. With the calibration parameters included, the unit-length matrix is far worse conditioned in several problems (for example Fujita and Isensee), because scaling factors of different observables can have nearly proportional sensitivities; this does not affect the selection, which conditions only the biological parameters.

\section*{Supplementary Note: Nonlinear check of the first-order problems}

In Alkan, Bachmann, Chen, Isensee, Lucarelli and Raimundez, admissibility was decided during the selection with the first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit,lin}}$. To check this decision, the final subset of each problem was re-estimated nonlinearly in the same 15 scenarios at $\pm$5\%, with exactly the settings used for the other problems: trust-region reflective least squares in $\log|\theta|$ within $[|\theta_0|/10,\,10|\theta_0|]$, two starting points ($\theta_0$ and the scenario value), at most $100(|S|+|\Theta_C|+1)$ function evaluations, and the value without re-estimation included (\texttt{analysis/efit\_nonlinear.py}; one process per scenario).

\emph{Results.} The nonlinear re-estimation confirmed admissibility in all six problems (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nonlinear}). In Alkan, Bachmann, Chen and Raimundez the nonlinear and first-order medians agree to within 0.002; in Isensee the nonlinear error is larger (0.113 against 0.076) but well below the threshold, with six valid scenarios. In Lucarelli the nonlinear median, 0.43585, lies $4\times10^{-5}$ below $\tau_e=0.43589$, with 8 of the 15 scenarios passing: its admissibility is confirmed but borderline, as stated in the main text. The computation took 21~h of computing time over the six problems, 13~h of them for Chen (35~s per simulation).

\section*{Supplementary Note: Selection on the training outputs, null controls and standard pivoted QR}

\emph{Purpose.} Three methodological issues affect the comparison of selection criteria. (i) Our method selected its subsets on all measured outputs, whereas the other criteria selected on the training outputs; the comparison may therefore favour our method in the tests that use held-out conditions. (ii) Without null baselines in the admissibility test, the fraction of admissible subsets of each criterion cannot be judged. (iii) A direct pivoting variant, column-pivoted QR of the normalised sensitivity matrix itself, was also evaluated, although it does not correspond to the standard Golub--Klema--Stewart subset-selection algorithm.

\emph{Design.} (i) The complete selection was repeated with the rows of the training set only (\texttt{selection.py} with \texttt{REPRO\_OUTPUTS=train}), with the fixed scenario set of the Methods; the resulting subsets were evaluated with the same admissibility test (on all outputs, $\pm$5\% and $\pm$20\%), the same real-data test and the same synthetic experiment (20 seeds) as the other criteria. (ii) The admissibility of re-estimating only the calibration parameters ($k=0$, without the rule $|S|\ge2$) and of 20 random $k$-subsets per problem (the subsets of the real-data random baseline, seeds 0--19) was evaluated at $\pm$5\% and $\pm$20\%. To keep the comparison consistent, the five criteria were re-evaluated with the same rule (fixed scenario set; first-order $e_{\mathrm{fit,lin}}=\min\{e_{\mathrm{rel}},\text{projection}\}$ per scenario). (iii) Standard pivoted QR (Golub, Klema and Stewart): SVD of the normalised training sensitivity matrix and column-pivoted QR of $V_k^{\top}$ (\texttt{qr\_selection.py} with \texttt{QR\_VARIANT=gks}). (iv) The synthetic comparison was repeated with log-uniform and sparse truths (Methods), ten seeds.

\emph{Results.} (i) \emph{Selection on the training outputs.} With the training outputs alone, 29 of the 34 problems have an admissible subset and 20 select a different subset from the main one, mostly another pair of parameters: in sloppy models many pairs suffice. Bertozzi has no admissible pair on the training outputs, although a single parameter (\texttt{R0\_CA}) reaches $e_{\mathrm{fit}}=0.08$, and Perelson cannot be evaluated because its training outputs change by less than the numerical noise floor. Evaluated on all outputs, these subsets were admissible in 24 of 27 comparable problems at $\pm$5\% and 23 of 27 at $\pm$20\%, against 28 and 27 of 28 for the main subsets: the main subsets owe part of their perfect score to being selected and tested on the same outputs. On real data their test $\chi^2/n$ did not differ from that of the main subsets (median ratio 1.00, Wilcoxon $P=0.17$). On synthetic data they reached $e_{\mathrm{pred}}\le\tau_e$ in 51\%, 48\% and 55\% of the cases for the three families of truths, not significantly different from Brun's (55\%, 50\% and 52\%) and significantly more often than profile likelihood and standard pivoted QR in every family (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:synthfamilies}).

(ii) \emph{Null controls.} Re-estimating the calibration parameters alone was admissible in 9 of 28 problems at both perturbation sizes, and random $k$-subsets in a median of 16 of 28 problems over the 20 draws (14--19 at $\pm$5\%, 14--18 at $\pm$20\%). In 16 problems at least half of the random draws are admissible, so in these models many subsets suffice, whereas in others, such as Lucarelli (0 of 20 draws admissible), the choice of parameters is decisive (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:admisaudit2}). Our main subsets (28 of 28), Brun's (26) and the training-output subsets (24 of 27) are well above both controls; Morris (23), profile likelihood (18 of 27) and standard pivoted QR (17) are close to the random baseline.

(iii) \emph{Standard pivoted QR.} The Golub--Klema--Stewart selection chose a different subset from the earlier variant in 23 of 29 problems. It was admissible in 17 of 28 problems at $\pm$5\% (earlier variant: 14) and 16 at $\pm$20\% (15), reached the synthetic threshold in 30\% of the uniform cases (34\%), and its real-data test error did not differ from ours (median ratio 1.00, $P=0.16$). The conclusions of the main text hold for both variants; the main text reports the standard one.

(iv) \emph{Other families of truths.} Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:synthfamilies} gives, for the three families, the success rate of every criterion and the difference from our method with 95\% confidence intervals from resampling problems. The ordering is the same in all three: our method and Brun's are not significantly different, and both are above profile likelihood and standard pivoted QR; the difference from Morris is significant for uniform and sparse truths but not for log-uniform ones.

\section*{Supplementary Note: Identifiability over the admissible set}

\emph{Purpose.} The hypothesis of \texttt{practically\_identifiable\_grad} on the nonlinear remainder must hold for every parameter vector of the admissible set (the box of the free parameters within the bounds of the study), whereas it had been checked only at the computed profile points. In addition, the classification of the profiles could depend on their resolution, and the subsets selected on the training outputs had not been profiled.

\emph{Design.} (a) The profiles of the subsets selected on the training outputs were computed with the same procedure, for the problems in which they differ from the main ones. (b) The profiles of the main subsets were recomputed with 20 displacements per side (from 0.02 to 4.6 in $\log|\theta_j|$, geometric spacing) instead of 10, from the same optimum. (c) Two variants of the theorem were proved in Lean (\texttt{practically\_identifiable\_grad\_tail} and \texttt{practically\_identifiable\_rel\_tail}): the remainder bound is needed only on the tail of the profile beyond the guaranteed half-width, either in absolute form ($\le\varepsilon$) or relative to the linear change ($\le\eta\|A\delta\|$, $\eta<1$). For every selected parameter and side, the tail bounds were estimated over the region of the free parameters within a factor $r$ of the optimum (and within the bounds of the study), for $r=10$, 5, 3, 2 and 1.5, from 512 Sobol points, the profile points of the region and an adversarial search (L-BFGS-B maximisation of the remainder or of the ratio on the tail, from the worst sampled points, repeated until the half-width found was stable; \texttt{analysis/identifiability\_box.py}). A parameter satisfies the hypotheses in the region when one of the two conditions holds on both sides.

\emph{Results.} (a) For the subsets selected on the training outputs, 35 of the 43 selected parameters were identifiable. (b) With twice as many profile points, the same 65 of 74 parameters were identifiable; the only change of category was \texttt{reaction\_6\_k1} of Fujita, from flat to non-identifiable on one side. (c) Over the region, the hypotheses held for 1 of the 74 parameters with $r=10$ (the whole range of the study), 0 with $r=5$, 3 with $r=3$, 7 with $r=2$ and 9 with $r=1.5$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:identifregion}; the non-monotone count between $r=10$ and $r=5$ reflects the independent sampling of each region). The Lean condition therefore supports practical identifiability only in a neighbourhood of the optimum, because the models are far from linear over a factor of ten in every free parameter; identifiability over the range of the study rests on the computed profiles. The sampled bounds are estimates of suprema, not proofs.
